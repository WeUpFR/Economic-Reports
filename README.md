The November report is a follow-up to the 28-30 October report on AI-enabled hacking and Trinidad and Tobago's financial sector ([first report](https://github.com/WeUpFR/Economic-Reports/blob/claude/gifted-tesla-5c581m/Trinidad_Financial_Sector_AI_Cyber_Readiness_2026-10-28.md), on its own branch). It covers the 18 days since: the FBI's attribution of the US$6.34 billion Binance theft, the Crédit Mutuel Arkéa and Mercado Bitcoin thefts, the first FBI and Google reports of autonomous AI attack frameworks, and a second Japanese email-vendor breach. It then sets out what METR's time-horizon data do and do not say about 2027, updates the first report's forecast and odds, and checks Trinidad and Tobago's progress against the first report's watch-list and deadlines, including the Central Bank's Financial Stability Report 2025 and TT-CSIRT's coverage of exploited flaws (13 of 241). It closes with the country's wider AI readiness, an updated scorecard and eight actions for the next 90 days, drawing on 102 sources. The HTML version has hover details on the charts; download it and open it in a browser.

The September report examines the relationship between stock prices and the economy, TTSE trading depth, institutional ownership, NIBTT cash needs and contribution reforms, possible buyers of institutional share sales, and conditional liquidity stress scenarios. It includes dated evidence, calculation methods and 18 sources.
