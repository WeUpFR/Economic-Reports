The October report reviews documented AI-enabled attacks from 2024 to 2026, the offensive capability of Chinese and other open-weight models, and Trinidad and Tobago's cyber incidents, regulation, law and shared payment infrastructure. The 29-30 October extension adds the latest attacks (the US Federal Election Commission, Japanese banks hit through an email vendor, and AI agent swarms), how fast AI cyber capability is growing, an institution-by-institution review of what more than 40 local financial institutions disclose about their defences, sector totals from passive checks of their public email-security settings, a test of whether national warning and response can keep pace with machine-speed attacks, and a dated update on the late-October "Black October" wave (the AI-found PHP2Shell flaws, Polymarket, US Defense Department incidents, strained incident responders, Haiti, and the record US$6.3 billion Binance theft). It closes with a 13-domain readiness scorecard, likely attack routes and priority actions, drawing on 191 sources. Every section was independently fact-checked against its cited sources before publication.

The September report examines the relationship between stock prices and the economy, TTSE trading depth, institutional ownership, NIBTT cash needs and contribution reforms, possible buyers of institutional share sales, and conditional liquidity stress scenarios. It includes dated evidence, calculation methods and 18 sources.
